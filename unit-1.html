Blog: More Than Just Baseball

My blog will explore how shared sports fandom can preserve a connection with someone even after they are gone.

I was watching the Chicago White Sox, a team my grandmother used to watch all the time. For the past few years, they had been one of the worst teams in Major League Baseball...

For the past few years, watching the Chicago White Sox has been pretty painful. They had become one of the worst teams in Major League Baseball, coming off two seasons with over 100 losses, including one of the worst seasons in modern MLB history. Going into this season, I honestly expected more of the same. Instead, something changed. They started winning, and for the first time in a while it felt like they could actually compete with some of the best teams in baseball.

The White Sox have always meant more to me because of my grandmother. She watched them all the time and stuck with them through both the good and bad seasons. One memory that stands out to me happened at my graduation party. Everyone was watching the White Sox play the Los Angeles Dodgers, who were the reigning World Series champions. The White Sox ended up beating them, and I remember looking over at my grandmother and seeing how happy she was. I do not think I had ever seen her smile that hard while watching a baseball game.

My grandmother also loved watching birds, and her favorite bird was a cardinal. Recently, she passed away, and since then watching the White Sox has felt different. When I watched them clinch a playoff spot for the first time since 2021, I screamed in my dorm. After watching so many awful seasons, they had finally made it back. But almost immediately I thought about my grandmother. She had seen the 2005 World Series team, and she had also watched through all of the terrible seasons that followed. I just wished she could have been there to see them finally reach the playoffs again.

After the game, I looked out my window and saw a bird land on the ledge. It was a cardinal. I know there is no way to prove that it meant anything, but I like to believe that my grandmother somehow saw that moment too. That experience made me realize that sports can become connected to people and memories in ways that go way beyond the actual game.

This led me to the phenomenon I want to explore: how shared sports fandom can help people keep a connection with someone after they are gone. Why can a sports team become so closely connected to one person? Why can a win or loss suddenly bring back memories that have nothing to do with the actual game? And why can certain symbols, like seeing a cardinal, feel so meaningful during moments like this? For me, the White Sox are no longer just a baseball team. They have become part of how I remember my grandmother and the moments we shared together.
